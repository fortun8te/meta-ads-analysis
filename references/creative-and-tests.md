# Creative and tests

## Inspect before explaining performance

Describe the media first, then interpret matching results. Keep observable execution separate from inferred persuasion. Record medium, aspect ratio and actual placement separately. A tall asset alone does not prove Reels delivery; a native-looking photo alone does not prove a real customer endorsement.

Use distinct tags:

- Concept family: stable buying motivation/message and intended customer situation.
- Angle: the persuasive reason to consider the product.
- Hook: the opening attention device or the first thing noticed in a static.
- Execution: visible story, scene, action, point of view and evidence device.
- Format/presentation: static/video/carousel and native/designed/hybrid, with a useful visible subtype.
- Offer, proof, product role, CTA, intended persona/awareness and destination.

Tag only what the source supports. An intended persona is not a verified delivered demographic. A new crop or color is an execution variant, not a distinct buying motivation. Maintain stable concept and parent/version IDs so results and production history remain traceable. Extend existing brand taxonomies before inventing a competing one.

## Static review

Open the whole image at useful detail and phone scale. Explain what grabs attention, reading order, scene/action, emotional tension, product visibility and its link to the full copy. Inspect overlays, proof/offer/CTA, legibility, credible objects/anatomy and packaging against the actual reference when product fidelity matters. Transcribe important image text separately from Meta copy; flag OCR uncertainty.

Assess message congruence: does the image promise what the copy and destination explain? Is the offer's qualification visible or contradicted? Aesthetic polish and engagement potential are separate from measured sales. A creative can be clear and policy-sensitive, or rough and effective; report those dimensions separately. Cosmetic defects matter when they impair understanding, credibility or the user's explicit standard.

## Video and carousel review

For video inspect opening, body sequence, proof/product reveal, transitions and ending CTA, with timestamps. Cover speech, audio, on-screen text, visual changes and silent/mobile viewing where relevant. Opening-only or transcript-only inspection supports only that scope. Pair watch metrics with the actually sampled section; a thumbnail cannot establish pacing or hook content.

For carousel inspect every available card in order, text/destination differences and the sequence's argument. Distinguish an individual asset observation from whole-ad performance. Dynamic combinations and personalized delivery remain incomplete unless directly inspected and reported at that scope.

## Explain patterns cautiously

Compare contrasting examples, not only the best-looking/highest-return rows. Look for repeated evidence across comparable contexts and reruns. A pattern that appears only in one low-volume ad is a candidate. Record failed and inconclusive versions too. Spend-weighted summaries describe delivered results; equal-per-ad summaries describe a different distribution. Neither identifies a causal visual effect.

When recommending a new variant, separate the source ad's measured result, any isolated feature evidence, your visual judgment and the untested prediction. Whole-ad success does not prove its scene, lighting, character or product gesture caused that result. A proposed version has no measured ranking until it runs under an interpretable comparison. For absence claims such as “we never tested outdoor,” state the inspected universe, ambiguous classifications, unique-asset versus ad-version count, and video coverage.

Useful diagnostic hypotheses include changing an opening when the sales argument appears promising, testing message/CTA timing when viewing is healthy but clicking is weak, or investigating offer/page congruence when attention is strong but purchases are weak. State alternative explanations and which missing evidence would distinguish them.

## Build the next test

Match the user's intent:

- A targeted fix preserves unaffected scene/copy/composition.
- A controlled iteration changes the specified variable and holds the chosen comparator stable.
- Broad concept exploration permits meaningful differences in motivation, action, information, viewpoint or story tension, subject to the brief's fixed elements.

There is no mandatory 80/20 or 70/30 split, number of creatives, refresh calendar or universal winning format. Own-brand evidence is the default where the user wants account-led iteration. Use customer language only from actual attributed comments, reviews, quiz answers or research; retain sampling/date limits. Keep testimonials, expert roles and claims grounded in real evidence.

For each proposed test record:

1. The observation, exact source ads/window and outcome counts.
2. The proposed mechanism and a plausible alternative explanation.
3. The change, fixed elements and a concrete brief that makes the difference visible.
4. Comparator, primary outcome and reporting/evaluation plan.
5. What supported, unsupported and inconclusive outcomes would teach us.

Prioritize decision value, evidence strength, distinctness, feasibility and current business objective. Missing coverage alone does not make an idea worthwhile. Keep predictions labelled first picks to test. Generate or edit assets only when requested and through the user's current production workflow. Record generated, inspected, approved, launched and performance-measured states separately.
